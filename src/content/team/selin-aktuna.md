Selin Aktuna is a Ph.D. candidate in the Neuroscience Program at the University of Illinois
Urbana-Champaign. Her research broadly focuses on understanding the underlying causes and
mechanisms of increased vulnerability to neurodegenerative disease and how environmental exposures,
sex and hormonal status, and genetic risk factors interact across the lifespan to influence brain
aging and Alzheimer's disease.

In the Gritton Lab, Selin investigates how early-life environmental exposures can shape long-term
susceptibility to Alzheimer's disease-related cognitive and neuropathological changes. A major
focus of her doctoral research examines the effects of prenatal exposure to phthalates, a class of
widespread endocrine-disrupting chemicals, in both wild-type and mouse models of AD. Her work
explores how these developmental exposures interact with sex, aging, and loss of ovarian hormones
to influence cognitive function and the emergence of Alzheimer's disease-related pathology later in
life.

Selin uses a multidisciplinary approach that combines behavioral neuroscience, neuropathology,
molecular biology, and neuroimaging. Her research incorporates behavioral assays alongside
immunohistochemical measures of neuroinflammation and AD pathology. She also integrates molecular
and systems-level approaches, including gene expression analyses, inflammatory profiling, and
neuroimaging measures such as PET and MRI, to better understand relationships between changes in
the brain and behavioral outcomes.

More broadly, Selin is interested in the intersection of developmental neurotoxicology, aging, and
neurodegeneration. Her long-term research interests center on understanding how experiences and
exposures early in life can alter the trajectory of brain aging decades later, with the goal of
identifying biological mechanisms that contribute to vulnerability or resilience to cognitive
decline and neurodegenerative disease.
