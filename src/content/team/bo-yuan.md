## Projects

### Striatal D1, D2 and cholinergic neurons in movement and reward

![Illustration of a head-fixed mouse on a spherical treadmill viewing a virtual environment, with calcium imaging and neuronal activity traces](/images/people/bo-yuan/striatal-movement-reward.png)

The striatum, the main input nucleus of the basal ganglia, integrates cortical and thalamic inputs and contributes to action selection, motor control and reward-related learning. This project focuses on three functionally distinct striatal cell types: D1 receptor–expressing and D2 receptor–expressing medium spiny neurons (D1-MSNs and D2-MSNs) and cholinergic interneurons (ChIs). Using single-photon calcium imaging in head-fixed mice navigating a virtual-reality (VR) environment for water rewards, we characterize how movement and reward modulate the activity of each cell type. Given that the loss of dopaminergic input is a hallmark of Parkinson’s disease, we further ask whether these neurons show altered patterns of movement and reward modulation in the parkinsonian state, with the aim of providing a cell-type-resolved account of striatal dysfunction in the disease.

### Reorganization of hippocampal CA1 networks during extinction

![Illustration of hippocampal calcium imaging, trace eyeblink conditioning, and changing patterns of activity across neuronal networks](/images/people/bo-yuan/ca1-network-extinction.png)

Extinction is the gradual weakening of a conditioned response when the conditioned stimulus (CS) is no longer followed by the unconditioned stimulus (US), and is generally thought to reflect new learning rather than elimination of the original memory. Combining hippocampus-dependent trace eyeblink conditioning with calcium imaging in mice, we track the same population of CA1 neurons as CS–US pairing gives way to presentation of the CS alone. Beyond changes in the activity of individual neurons, we examine reorganization at the network level: how the composition of the highly active ensemble is redistributed as extinction proceeds. We further distinguish excitatory from inhibitory neurons and compare the distinct roles they play in reorganizing the CA1 network.

### calcia: a physics-based simulator for calcium imaging

Evaluating methods for cell extraction and spike inference in calcium imaging is fundamentally limited by the lack of ground truth in real recordings. calcia addresses this by simulating one-photon (widefield or miniscope) and two-photon calcium imaging from first principles. It generates synthetic tissue containing vasculature, somata and dendrites, drives a calcium-binding model with indicator saturation from spike trains, and renders movies through microscope-specific optics, including point-spread functions, tissue scattering and blood absorption, followed by motion artifacts and detector noise. Because the position and calcium activity of every cell are known exactly, calcia provides a quantitative benchmark for methods that extract cells and spikes from calcium imaging data.
