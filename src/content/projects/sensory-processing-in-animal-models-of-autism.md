One of the most common phenotypes present in individuals with autism includes sensory hypersensitivity and difficulty hearing in complex sensory environments. Genetic studies have revealed several genetic mutations that are highly correlated with ASD.

In our lab we use a mouse model of Fragile X Syndrome, the Fmr1-knockout mouse line, to study sensory encoding in complex environments. Using a novel task designed by this lab, we show that FMR1-KO animals and WT animals can perform an auditory discrimination task equally as well in a low attentional load environment. However, increasing the attentional load via either auditory or visual distractors causes the Fmr1-KO mice to be unable to perform the task at a high level.

Currently we are investigating how the cholinergic system could potentially play a role in the attentional deficits that we see in the Fmr1-KO mice.
