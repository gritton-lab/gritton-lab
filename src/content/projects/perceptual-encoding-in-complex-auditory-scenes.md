A cocktail party, filled with sounds coming  simultaneously from multiple directions, exemplifies how complex acoustic stimuli compete for our attention. The biological auditory system has evolved to manage this complexity effectively. However, the specific mechanisms by which the auditory cortex successfully suppresses competing stimuli so that attended streams are preferentially represented remain largely unknown.

One of the projects in the lab aims to uncover the mechanisms of attention and cortical inhibition that help isolate specific sound streams from background noise within the auditory cortex. To address this challenge, we employ a combination of cutting-edge techniques. These include novel imaging and electrophysiology methods to monitor neural activity in the auditory and prefrontal cortices in vivo, optogenetics to selectively control the activity of different neuronal populations, and machine learning algorithms designed to quantify how neural populations encode and discriminate sounds arising from different spatial locations .

Through this integrative approach, we aim to uncover the neural mechanisms underlying auditory attention, cortical inhibition, and spatial sound processing in cocktail-party listening environments.
